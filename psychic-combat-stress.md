# The psychological results of considering gearing for combat
## BIG DISCLAIMER!!
As a God-fearing man, I do not condone random violence, cultism, nor terrorism, even as the military propaganda machine does tend to induce fear in the foes of the military industrial complex. I follow the law as the Constitution intended, not as the cabal wants me to. However, that does not make me a terrorist or willing criminal, because for many, trying to be sane is being criminalized. If you wish to contest this, please take it up with the government to see who you can and cannot criticize. I am not a military son, and I am not a soldier. I am a civilian who has decided to take up arms, and am thus a combatant under the Geneva Convention. From here, you may refer to me as Survivor King Senki, and not by my birth name; and no, you may not make a personality cult out of me.
### On with the show!
And no, I'm not talking about fighting Iran or any of these shitholes where violence reigns. I mean gearing up to fight the tyrants here on my home soil. My revolution is to follow my moral code, no matter what, knowing full well that tyrants and criminals will seek to kill me and sever my connection to God.

The humans that lead this nation, knowing that I say this as a human myself, are irrecoverably corrupt and evil. They wish to kill access to information, kill the uniqueness of many cultures including our own, and force us to follow their lethal will. As such, becoming a cyberpunk of God is far more important than anything else.

I've... Never considered participating in conflict before. It's a foreign concept to me, though I've played many games with conflict as the main theme, whether as a commander or as a warrior. Now that I'm considering spilling real blood, or at least leaving bruises on thieves, I've been more than a little scared. It's also led me to consider the origin points of my blood.

### A psychic's mind is on fire
One front that I have access to is my interaction with psychic power. I've been developing it off and on, enough to get a general read on the overall mental state of towns, as well as the sorrow of a good friend of mine who lost his father to illness. It's also been a source of pain however; I can also feel the grief of constant warfare and am often paralyzed by it for minutes on end. Before this I found that shutting my emotions out worked temporarily, especially in games like Destiny 2 where one wrong move would send you back to respawn in PVP, same as Halo. It's also quite limiting as logic alone cannot account for all potential incidents.

From there, I developed a primal mindset, where wrath and fury lived in harmony with peace and happiness. My mind had become far more flexible that way, but I unintentionally shut out my logic in the process. That's when I discovered my berserkergang, access to a perfected state of mind that, as its cost, reduces my ability to discern friend from foe, and leaves me mentally exhausted for days to a week. Now everything seems to have fallen into place. But then it hit me: I'm still not mentally prepared to spill blood if needed. I still dread that day, knowing that who I am now, unless my mind is saved by God, will die and a far more willing persona will take my place.

Nonetheless, I gather my resolve. I rebuke the people who say there is no pure good or evil, for I see both sides in the midst of the scale, and they would both break it. I rebuke the devils who constantly harrass me and attempt to decimate my family's cohesion. I rebuke the demons who resolved to kill Charlie Kirk in the most cowardly way possible. Same with the victims of Waco and Ruby Ridge.

I am a berserker shaman. Both Heaven and Earth have no place for the evil of monsters and demons. Fighting to eat, fighting to stay alive, that is right in the natural world. For us, we want to avoid killing each other and not subvert the cultures of others, so long as they feel the same. Fighting to commit xenocide, destroying yet unborn children and using cults to subvert others, are some of the actions that should be utterly forbidden, and indeed they will give you tickets to Hell if you don't repent soon enough.

God, I have but one request. If Valhalla exists like the old Germanic lore believed, I wish to go there; the job of a good man, a God-fearing man, is never complete, and I will fight and die to see humanity at peace once more.
### Guns guns guns!
Who said cyberpunks were better with tech than with guns? I said that, rage is an excellent motivator. But seriously, while classic cyberpunks were exploring the web and various networks, both public and private, fictional cyberpunks were just as proficient with guns as with their cyberdecks. From pistols to sniper rifles and back with shotguns, while everyone had their preferences, they were still good with whatever iron was given to them, even swords, because those would come back if they were made to work against modern armor. As such, I should pick a gun that is known reliable, and a gun that is rather reliable as a special weapon, like a sniper rifle or shotgun.

### Going with style
In order for you and your psyche to survive this world, you need to be the main character of your story. In short:

1. You need to survive
2. You want to make your mark, good or bad
3. Be yourself; no one will tell you that you must change without being dismissed.

As such, I looked to Hackers for inspiration. Rather, the movie found me and inspired me to do research.

Dade's purple combat vest was a perfect fit for me, so I decided to get a combat vest of my own once I get the money. No frilly skirts nor devious costumes like I was thinking of in peacetime. I did a proof of concept with my winter vest and my shirt-turned-shoulder sash, which houses a knife in the knot. I can definitely pull it off, and I can store ammunition and written matters of importance.

Next was cargo pants, especially warcore. Many of them are expensive, so I decided to settle for regular cargo pants with more pockets than I have fingers on one hand. They'll carry other important stuff like a leg-pocket IFAK, and maybe a tourniquet. Another matter regarding the pants was them not tearing pockets off so easily when I find myself in the brush, which I found that my cheap pair that I had the time succeeded in.

I have a digital BDU in my possession, and I've decided to wear it for maneuvers and combat. When out of combat, I'll have my vest on still, both for the style and for the consistent practicality of it. I think it's US Army make. It's got wrist pockets for critical information and shoulder pads to prevent scuffs and sleeve destruction when diving. It also has a breathable design, which was one of the major things the US military wanted to address after using the old battle uniforms and jackets.

Storage, that's another conundrum entirely. Thankfully it's a solved problem since I have my old school backpack and, unfortunately, one part of the puzzle that is my Versahunt pack. I'll remind myself here that items that I must access quickly must be near the top of the pack, bag or other container.

All of this would give off the appearance of a knocksteady modern warrior who knows what he needs at all times. However, there's a hitch. I'm without tech in this general setup.
### The tech behind being a cyberpunk
Technology has gone through an insane revolution ever since the 2000s, and now more than ever, an optimized system can and will beat out most corporate and user-friendly FOSS software. However, that's currently out of my reach, so currently I have MX Linux and Windows 7 installed on my laptop, as well as Armbian Ubuntu on my Orange Pi PC, set to load a full power Macintosh HDFD emulated through Mini vMac, prepackaged with a bunch of development stuff including Hypercard. On Windows 7, I also have Decker installed.

With this, I can do everything I need to, from making protestware to zines, from administrative control to hacking tools. If I get a third SSD before things go south (or if the internet is still on afterwards), I'll get Kali Linux to provide basic security and digital combat capabilites. My iPhone is jailbroken and my Kyocera (which I'll use in harsh environments) is rooted, so both can have their respecive telemetry stripped out, unlike newer phones. Programs are still being published for their respective latest versions as well.

Cyberdeck-wise, I think I'll make it a luggable computer, with peripherals and internal extensions used by my Le Potato, with a case inside to protect it further, and some extra protection against impacts for said extensions. Power will be wired, to support external power packs. Casing and supports will be made with ABS flats. This is actually going to be my take on the Virtuscope, something I put up a project on over at Cyberdeck Cafe.

As for peripherals... In a lawless place like this, included tools should be an SDR unlocked to access all frequencies, range extenders, and the requisite software, such as JS8Call (for GhostNet reporting) and other forms of communication. I would host a TOR node free of nonsense if I'm in one place for a time, as well as a Gemini Capsule, in order to boost my solo protests and communication options. Maybe I can use a simple web server to host DIRC on TOR to further drill home the redundancy. To do all this, the GUI will have to be minimal, and I will have to use mostly text-mode programs via terminals or services.
### What I've already done
1. Set up laptop with Win7 and MX Linux on separate SSDs
2. Load up Orange Pi PC with Macintosh System 6.0.8 via Mini vMac, preloaded with development tools including HyperCard
3. Jailbroken/rooted all 3 phones in my possession (Kyocera and iPhone SE will be used, Casio reserved for games)
4. Started learning, and continuing to learn programming
5. Found and downloaded Hobby OSes that will be of use when all else fails
6. Acquired a Nostromo N52 to handle macros and shortcuts.
