# The stark difference between "Augmenting Human Intellect" and "Artificial Intelligence"

I feel that I must address this for the people in the clanker crowd who believe that Doug Engelbart's push to improve the human mind somehow ties into the AI rush of the 90s. The only real connection between these is how Xerox and Xerox PARC pulled in people from the two competing Lisp Machine companies and some members of Engelbart's bootstrapping crusade to kickstart their own. The AI craze faded while human intellect boosters like the Apple Lisa became the gold standard for user interfaces.

To nail this home. I'll start with Artificial Intelligence.
  
### Artificial Intelligence
AI are generally referred to as thinking machines, sentient machines or, more bluntly, large language models and image generators. The goal of an AI developer is to make a system that can think and process information without a human's input, save for answering questions from the human user. Whether it's in text, voice, or generated images, it will do this, and currently with a high chance of hallucinating.

The Turing Test was, while AI-related, moreso a general purpose assessment that attests that a computer can do everything logic-wise that a human can. This was first proven with one of Sinclair's machines I recall (though I'm American, I have great interest in 8-bit micros and other European computer eras). That same test is what most computers need to pass today.

### Augmenting Human Intelligence
Instead of making a machine think for you, it instead provides easy access and editing capabilities to any sort of information you may want to store. To quote the NLS User Training Guide: *"Let us take the case of a researcher using NLS to both gather his information and present it in final printed reports. Every tool the researcher needs to produce camera ready copy of his reports is available through NLS. He may research information stored and catalogued in the computer, use the system to keep notes or organize his material, and communicate with his fellow-workers through the computer..."*

Long story short, the concept is an extremely advanced and large library that can be added to, internally modified and removed from. You don't even need to print anything if you're a computer-only user. All the information is still there at your fingertips.

When you put two and two together, between the Xerox Lisp Machines, the Apple Lisa and the NLS, you get what we have been using for a long time until recently, since Windows 11 and other operating systems seek to shake up the user's landscape with extremely optimized user interfaces and minimal interactivity focusing on exactly the single thing that companies want users to do.

My markdown environment project is an attempt at replicating this experience, not to hand over to a measely LLM for it to hallucinate constantly.
