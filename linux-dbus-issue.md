# DBus and its wide connection to many Linux distros
If you're reading this, I'm not dead, but you may have heard about the nonsensical age verification laws that are being passed right now. This time, they're targeting Linux. Why? That's one of the few major bastions left of software freedom that they want to snuff out. That's the one place where they can't stick their fingers into, even if they have gotten an initial foothold via Red Hat. To do this, their co-conspirators on the inside are looking to DBus.

DBus is THE general-purpose cross-program signal sender for the major Linux distros out there: Debian, Arch Linux, GmbH Manjaro, Gentoo, OpenSUSE, Fedora, you get the picture, it's everywhere where there's also SystemD, which handles services and other stuff an init system is not meant to handle. Ubuntu and ElementaryOS are turning to it to trigger a mass unintentional adoption of their new "age bracket" API, which makes this far more sinister than just an operating system feeling like they can't fight back against the entire government of an entire state who betray their people on the daily like it's breakfast, lunch and dinner. I'll spell it out for you.

**DBus accepting this change in the upstream will trigger a chain reaction of either unintended adoption or forks preventing it from being applied.**

This is one of the biggest examples of an "opt-out" that's hard to opt out from without consequences. Everyone who forks will have to maintain compatibility with newer versions while keeping that API **OUT** as it gets shuffled around between iterations and gets backed by government and military pressure. This is a fucking **WAR** we're fighting with nothing to hand but guerilla warfare.

Here's what I've done so far. Windows 7 doesn't have any updates to hand anymore, even with ESU enabled, so it's safe. So is every Windows version before it. Older Linux versions are safe because they're no longer updated, but due to the open source nature, someone can take up said projects and force-feed a form of the API that way without anyone knowing. That would be downright sabotage. As for newer Linux distros, you can just grind updates to a halt, and make them crash into a wall if need be.

As for embedded operating systems and related electronics, no doubt once it hits them a major lawsuit or two will be launched against all parties interested in age verification. Brazilians are actively committing crimes just to use more reasonable operating systems. Pretty soon I'll be doing the same as an Arizonan. Ageless Linux seems to have given the engine of revolution the kick it needs.
